1. TL;DR — one page

What the demo is: a trigger-driven, resident agent-cluster runtime. On trigger it snapshots real US market data as the initial environment, instantiates 96 manually defined agents (64 LLM personas + 22 compiled-policy agents + 7 rule agents + 3 mechanism/meta roles), runs a multi-asset market through discrete batch call auctions, and delivers relative differences across intervention variants (dispersion, spreads, depth, tail risk, flow concentration) — never point predictions of price direction.

Five core design decisions (carried from v1, unchanged):

  1. Participants are a weighted panel, not a headcount. ~160M US adults own stocks but the top 1% of households holds ~half of equities. 96 agents represent the market; AUM/flow weights carry scale. Ratios are coarse in the demo and tighten when real-person calibration arrives (Phase 2).
  2. Three-tier cognition (LLM persona / compiled policy / pure rule) — the structural reason cluster cost < N × individual agent (§3.2, §8).
  3. Decision/matching separation: the LLM emits Intent only; a deterministic execution wrapper converts it to legal orders; validators enforce budget/information/mandate constraints (BP validation layer 2).
  4. Batch call auction per round (1 sim-minute/round), continuous LOB deferred to v1+ — controllable LLM call counts, structural kinship with the SSW experimental market, no HFT millisecond rabbit hole.
  5. Regulators are the mechanism layer + event agents, not proportional participants — they occupy rule space (circuit breakers, LULD, halts, per-asset regulator map in §4.3), not ownership share.
Red lines inherited from the BP (unchanged): ① model output is not evidence — all market-level demo results are pipeline validation only; reproducing fat tails is a smoke test, not validity. ② multi-model × multi-prompt sensitivity is itself a deliverable: if conclusions flip under prompt changes, that instability goes into the report.

v1 computed this carefully; v2 keeps it as context that motivates archetype choice and coarse weights — no longer as a calibration target, per the user's "rough proportions are enough for the demo".

Right: volume participation by caliber 【estimate】 — rows use different calibers and must not be summed; HFT/market-making is the intermediary side of everyone else's flow.
  • Ownership concentration is the reason a small panel works: top 1% households ≈ 50% of corporate equity; top 10% ≈ 85–87% (Fed DFA). Behaviorally, ~160M holders reduce to a handful of archetypes × weights.
  • Institutional side is mandate-driven and highly homogeneous: ~8.7k mutual funds (ICI), ~15.8k SEC-registered RIAs, ~4k hedge funds — a dozen archetypes cover most flow styles.
  • Demo consequence: keep 6 retail + 4 institutional LLM archetypes; coarse weights: household-linked ≈ 40%, institutions ≈ 40%, foreign ≈ 15%, mechanical (index/buyback) ≈ 5% — close enough to Fig 1 for demo purposes, tightened in Phase 2.

Fig 2 · Three-tier cognition and escalation paths. Cost per decision differs by ~3 orders of magnitude across tiers. Core principle: personas are cheap to store and expensive to think — so they think rarely and reuse compiled habits.
This is behavior modeling, not just cost engineering: real investors do not re-derive their strategy every minute either — habit + trigger is the realistic default, deep deliberation the exception. Attention gating simultaneously satisfies the BP principle that simulated investors must exhibit friction, hesitation, and delay.

4.4 Cross-asset coupling: endogenous, not hard-coded

  • Channel 1 — shared event stream: one macro announcement (Fed statement A/B) hits every asset simultaneously; each persona carries a per-asset sensitivity vector.
  • Channel 2 — cross-asset agents: macro HF, CTA, risk-parity-style, and foreign-institution personas rebalance across books, propagating a rates shock into equities/FX (v1).
  • Channel 3 — balance-sheet constraints: margin calls on futures losses force sales elsewhere (v1, with margin accounting).
  • Deliberately absent: engine-level no-arbitrage links or fixed correlations. Cross-asset correlation must emerge from agent behavior — consistent with the BP thesis that endogenous reaction chains are the product; hard-wired structure would fake it.
What multi-asset buys the wind-tunnel story: the policy-communication scenario stops being "did stocks go up" and becomes "how did the joint distribution of rate/equity dispersion and vol respond to wording A vs B" — exactly the BP's deliverable of relative risk differences, now with a cross-asset tail-risk dimension.

6.2 Endogenous vs exogenous (unchanged)

  • Exogenous: news/announcement content and timing (trigger-time snapshot + scenario scripts); short-rate path.
  • Endogenous: prices, volumes, spreads, depth, flows, crowding, stampedes — the intervention's transmission chain (announcement → agent reaction → orders → price → other agents' reaction) is fully endogenous.
  • Smoke tests only: baseline runs should qualitatively reproduce fat tails, vol clustering, volume-|return| correlation. Per the BP these are pipeline health checks, not validity evidence.

8.3 Why cluster < N × individual (unchanged, four structural sources)

  1. Most agents are dormant most of the time — an always-on per-agent loop pays ~250× for "available at any moment".
  2. Most decisions are routinizable — habit/trigger policies (Tier B) handle the bulk; the LLM thinks only on OOD states.
  3. Computation is shared — the KV-cached prefix is the shared world model; continuous batching amortizes one GPU across all concurrent personas. This is the definitional difference between a cluster and N independent processes.
  4. Personas are cheap to store, expensive to think — 64 cards < 100KB; compiled habits make marginal behavior cost approach Tier-C zero.

Plus cross-run amortization: the wind tunnel runs the same population through scenario variants — persona compilation, policy cache, and memos all carry over, so variant #2..k cost far less than variant #1.

12. Risks & open items

  • LLM "textbook rationality" bias【fact: Henning et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.15800】— default-prompted LLM traders are too rational, bubbles too weak. Countermeasure: persona cards explicitly inject friction (information gaps, hesitation, delay, disposition parameters); friction strength is a calibration knob, currently a prior【assumption】.
  • Small-panel concentration risk: 64 LLM personas sharing one 8B model's priors may produce more homogeneous reactions than real populations. Monitor: reaction-dispersion vs the heterogeneity lower bounds from SCF/NFCS.
  • 8B capacity ceiling: if intent quality on novel-event rounds is weak (measurable via validator + manual spot checks on D4–7), the gateway promotes those calls to a larger model — routing is per-call, so this is a threshold change, not a redesign.
  • Multi-asset thinness: with 96 agents, each non-EQ book has few natural participants; v1 needs the panel to grow at least proportionally to book count, or books will be quote-dominated (MM-only) and unrealistic.
  • Call-auction fidelity: magnet-effect scenarios are sensitive to continuity assumptions; if that scenario is chosen as the headline, decide before D11 whether to pull continuous LOB forward.
  • All cost numbers are estimates until D8–10 measures them; every waterfall bar gets a measured counterpart on the dashboard.
  • Open (user decision pending): headline scenario for the first demo artifact; Phase-2 human-data source; nothing locks the schema to any locale.
